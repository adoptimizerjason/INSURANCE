## 1. Executive Summary & Direct Answers

| Your Question | Short Answer |
|---|---|
| **Family First Life (FFL)?** | Life-focused IMO under Integrity Marketing Group. **Pros:** you own your book / vested day one, high commission levels, free to join, fast releases. **Cons:** heavy recruiting/MLM culture, you self-fund expensive (often recycled) leads, downline chargebacks roll *up* to you, "commission level" naming can mislead. Best for self-driven life/final-expense sellers with capital. |
| **HealthMarkets?** | Large captive/semi-captive agency (health, Medicare, life, supplemental). **Pros:** 100+ carriers, strong structured training, good beginner on-ramp. **Cons:** you generally do **NOT** keep your book, agency takes a commission split, reported difficulty getting releases. Best as a paid apprenticeship if you accept losing the book. |
| **Independent / your own FMO?** | Maximum control and economics; you own everything. No salary, no built-in training, you fund all leads; your own direct FMO contracts need a multi-million-dollar production track record. Best as the destination, not the start. |

**Decision filter for the entire plan: whoever owns the renewals owns the business.** FFL and a true independent path keep the book yours. HealthMarkets, per the weight of agent reviews, does not. That single fact outweighs nearly every other feature.

**Net recommendation:** Sell **both** lines (Medicare/Med Supp for the renewal base, final expense/life for cash velocity). Start **independent under a rigorously vetted FMO** that lets you keep your book — FFL is viable for life/FE if you go in clear-eyed; for Medicare prefer a book-keeping Medicare FMO over a captive. Treat **own-FMO** as a Stage-3 endpoint after 2–4 years of production.

## 3. Licensing — Nevada Requirements & Costs

No "FMO license" exists — FMO is a contractual status with carriers, not a state credential.

**Steps:** (1) optional pre-licensing course (~$40–$200); (2) pass Nevada exam via Pearson VUE within one year before applying, combo Life/Health exam ~$47–$65; (3) digital fingerprints/background within 90 days (~$40–$60); (4) apply via Sircon — state fee **$185**, add a line **$50**; (5) get appointed by a carrier before selling; (6) 30 hrs CE / 3 yrs incl. 3 ethics hrs.

| Item | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|
| Pre-licensing (Life+Health combo) | $40 – $200 |
| Exam fee (combo) | ~$47 – $65 |
| State license (Sircon) | $185 |
| Fingerprints / background | ~$40 – $60 |
| **Total to get licensed** | **~$310 – $510** |

> Get the **combined Life & Health** line from day one. Confirm the current exam passing score in the official Nevada Candidate Handbook (some prep sites cite an 80% standard).

**Business entity (agency):** $185 + $50 per Designated Responsible Licensed Producer (DRLP); requires Nevada SOS registration and a named licensed DRLP.

## 4. Health vs. Life — The Core Product Decision

| Dimension | Health / Medicare | Life Insurance |
|---|---|---|
| Income shape | Flat, renewal-weighted, repeats yearly | Front-loaded; big Year 1, thin renewals |
| Cash velocity | Slower ramp; annuity-like base | Fast; strong month pays now |
| Commission cap | Yes — CMS hard maximum | No government cap |
| Seasonality | Intense (AEP) | Year-round |
| Regulation | Heavy — CMS, mandatory annual AHIP/NABIP (≥85%) | Lighter — state regs, suitability |
| Book durability | Strong — renewals carry you | Weaker trailing tail |
| Legal risk 2025–26 | Elevated (DOJ kickback litigation, CMS rule flux) | Lower, more stable |

**2026 Medicare commission maximums (Nevada / "all other states"):** MA initial **$694**/member/yr, renewal **$347**; Part D initial **$114**, renewal **$57**; referral fees $100 MA / $25 PDP. Carriers may pay less, never more; renewals capped at 50% of FMV. *Med Supp is NOT CMS-capped — carrier-set %, longer renewal tail.*

**Life/final-expense typical (uncapped):** Term/whole life Yr 1 ~50–100%+, renewals ~2–5%; final expense Yr 1 ~80–120% on small policies, modest renewals; annuities ~1–7% one-time.

**Verdict:** Sell **both**, sequenced. Medicare/Med Supp = recurring base; final expense/life = cash velocity. This affects the company choice: FFL is life/FE-centric; HealthMarkets is health/Medicare-centric.

## 6. DEEP DIVE: Family First Life

**What it is:** Life-focused Independent Marketing Organization (IMO), founded 2013 by Shawn Meaike, now under Integrity Marketing Group. Specializes in mortgage protection, final expense, life, and annuities. MLM-style hierarchy — earn on personal sales plus, if you recruit, on a downline.

### FFL — Pros
- **You own your book — vested 100% day one.** Consistently confirmed across reviews and FFL's own material. The single biggest structural advantage over a captive.
- **High commission levels** — advertised 95%–145% of annual premium; "best comp in the industry" is the common refrain (with caveats below).
- **Free to join; no agent-side FFL contract; FFL takes no cut of your personal commission** (you're paid by the carrier).
- **Fast releases** — frequently ~24–48 hours, far better than many captives.
- **Large lead marketplace** via Integrity Lead Center; no requirement to buy leads.
- **Training/culture infrastructure** — free events, boot camps, weekly calls, top-producer mentorship.
- **Large publicized bonus pools** (personal and agency production).
- **Multi-carrier** (Americo, Mutual of Omaha, etc.); path to override/agency income.

### FFL — Cons
- **Heavy recruiting / "MLM" culture** — many reviews describe a recruitment-first, "cult"-like environment; biggest income climb comes from recruiting.
- **You self-fund leads; quality contested** — frequent reports of expensive, recycled, non-exclusive leads ("you're the 10th to call"), low aged-lead close rates, pressure to buy internally.
- **Downline chargebacks roll UP to you** — if a recruit takes advances and bails, the debt rolls to you; one reviewer cited a leader absorbing $150K+ in roll-up.
- **"Commission level" naming misleads** — a "140 schedule" is a *name*, not a 1.4× payout; top comp often only on specific products, with competitive/FE products 10–30% lower. Demand the actual product grid.
- **Top levels need scale** — 140%+ generally needs ~$1M+ annual production.
- **No salary/benefits; feast-or-famine; ~90% industry first-year failure cited.**
- **Release nuance** — despite "no contracts," some report a ~6-month carrier write-block and inability to move until chargeback debt clears.
- **Upline lottery** — "choose your upline wisely" is the single most-repeated piece of advice.

**FFL bottom line:** Legitimate, large, book ownership is real. Best fit: you want life/final expense, are self-driven, have **$5–10K** lead/runway capital, thick skin, and you vet your specific upline hard. Worst fit: undercapitalized, want structure/salary, or recruiting-averse.

## 7. DEEP DIVE: HealthMarkets

**What it is:** Large, established agency (North Richland Hills, TX) distributing health, Medicare, life, and supplemental insurance with 100+ carriers. Operates closer to a captive/semi-captive model — carrier contracts generally held *through* HealthMarkets.

### HealthMarkets — Pros
- **Very broad product shelf** — 100+ carriers, strong cross-sell breadth.
- **Structured training & systems** — onboarding, carrier training, software/CRM repeatedly praised; genuinely good for a true beginner.
- **Established brand & long-tenure paths** — some agents report decades-long careers.
- **Residual commissions and bonuses** for sustained producers.
- **Internal leadership/advancement track.**
- **More lead structure than FFL** (still paid, but matched/structured programs).
- **Strong consensus it teaches the trade**, even among critics.

### HealthMarkets — Cons
- **You generally do NOT keep your book** — the most repeated, most serious criticism; contracts/clients reported to stay with HealthMarkets after you leave. Core structural disadvantage.
- **Commission split** — agency takes a cut (one detailed account: ~30% life/health/annuity, ~50% P&C; "pays half" of market Medicare/ancillary is a recurring claim).
- **Captive/restrictive** — "micromanaged," "W-2 feel without W-2 benefits," contracts only through them.
- **Reported difficulty getting releases** — multiple accounts of long fights to retrieve contracts (inverse of FFL).
- **Advance "trap"** — advances described as a retention lever.
- **Lead cost/quality complaints**; agents report being ignored once below top-producer status.
- **Commission-only, no base**; "lots of meetings."
- **Some BBB/consumer and "toxic environment" complaints** (weigh by volume vs. anecdote).

**HealthMarkets bottom line:** A reasonable paid-apprenticeship to learn health/Medicare with strong systems and the broadest shelf — *if* you accept the book likely isn't yours and the split is real. Dominant agent advice: use it to learn, then go independent. If book ownership matters (it should), prefer an independent Medicare FMO that lets you keep your book over a captive from the start.

## 8. DEEP DIVE: Independent / Build Your Own FMO

**What it is:** Contracting as your own entity — first as an independent agent under a strong FMO/upline, eventually with your own direct top-level FMO carrier contracts.

### Independent — Pros
- **You own everything** — book, renewals, clients, brand; fully portable.
- **Best economics** — street-level+ commissions, no agency split.
- **Full carrier choice** — no agency gate.
- **Own-FMO upside** — at scale, capture overrides on personal *and* downline production; income compounds beyond your hours. How the largest incomes are built.
- **No captivity** — no non-competes, book forfeiture, or release fights.

### Independent — Cons
- **Zero structure by default** — no salary, no built-in training/systems unless your FMO provides them.
- **You fund 100% of leads & overhead** from day one.
- **Own-FMO contracts require scale** — carriers demand a production promise (often **several million** annual) plus track record; unattainable at launch.
- **Downline liability** — responsible for downline conduct/advances; chargeback roll-up risk, fully yours.
- **Capital & cash-flow risk** — advancing commissions, funding leads, fixed costs before overrides arrive.
- **You become a manager** — running an FMO is recruiting/compliance/operations, not selling.
- **Medicare compliance exposure** — 2025–26 DOJ kickback litigation and CMS rule uncertainty.

### Realistic Sequence
```
Stage 0 (optional): Captive/structured start (e.g., HealthMarkets) — ONLY to learn,
        |  accept book loss as tuition; skip if you have sales skill + runway
        v
Stage 1: Independent under a strong FMO — keep your book (FFL-style for life;
        |  a vetted book-keeping FMO for Medicare)
        v
Stage 2: Build a small downline as a GA under your upline — override economics,
        |  lower direct exposure, no carrier volume promise yet
        v
Stage 3: Your own direct FMO contracts — ONLY if you can promise carrier volume
                                          AND want to run distribution
```

## 10. How to Vet Any FMO/Upline Before Signing

Get **written** answers — applies to FFL uplines, HealthMarkets, any FMO:

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Do I own my book? Vested? Day one? In writing? | Decisive — FFL yes; HealthMarkets reviews say no |
| Exact release terms — timeline, write-blocks, debt-clearance? | FFL fast but possible 6-mo block; HealthMarkets slow |
| Show me the **actual product commission grid**, not the schedule name | FFL "140" is a name, not 1.4x |
| What % does the agency take from my commission? | HealthMarkets ~30%+ reported; a true IMO takes none |
| Lead cost, exclusivity, refund policy, allowed vendors? | "Exclusive" often isn't; recycled leads = top complaint |
| If I recruit, whose chargebacks roll up to me? | Roll-up debt has bankrupted agents |
| Full carrier list I can contract with? | Determines ability to serve clients |
| Training, CRM, back-office actually included free? | Separates a real FMO from a logo |

> **Universal red flags:** No written book-ownership/vesting; pressure to sign now; refusal to show the product grid; vague/punitive release language; "exclusive" leads that aren't.

## 16. Bottom-Line Recommendations

1. **Product:** Life + Health combo license. Lead with Medicare (MA + PDP + Med Supp); layer final expense + life. The combination is the strategy, not a hedge.
2. **Structure:** Start independent under a rigorously vetted, book-keeping FMO. FFL is viable for life/FE if you accept its lead/culture realities. Use HealthMarkets only as a short paid apprenticeship if you have no sales background — knowing you likely give up the book.

# PART II — MARKETING & LEAD-GENERATION PLAN

*How to generate your own leads, own the funnel, and stop overpaying for marked-up leads*

---

## 17. The Core Strategy: Own Your Funnel

You diagnosed the problem correctly. When an FMO or agency sells you "their" leads, the lead vendor sells the same data, the FMO marks it up, and you take 100% of the cost and the chargeback risk. The vendor and the upline win whether you close or not.

**The strategic answer is a barbell, not one tactic:**

- **Front foot (own assets):** Build a branded, search-and-referral-fed funnel you control — a website + a quiz funnel + lead magnets + a nurture system + a referral engine. These leads are *exclusive by definition* (nobody else has them), cost less per acquisition over time, and are an asset that compounds (and that you own, like your book).
- **Back foot (bought volume, surgically):** Use bought leads — but only **direct from the source vendor, never the FMO markup**, and only the *types* that actually convert (live transfers/inbound calls and tightly-filtered fresh exclusive), to fill capacity while your owned funnel matures.

The mistake 90% of failing agents make: 100% bought shared/aged leads, no owned asset, no nurture, no referral system. They rent their pipeline forever and quit when the math turns negative.

**Your rule:** every dollar should either (a) buy a high-intent conversation today, or (b) build an asset that produces conversations forever. Never spend on anything that does neither.

---

## 18. The Economics — Why Bought Leads Bleed You

The number that matters is **not cost per lead (CPL). It is cost per acquisition (CPA) = CPL ÷ (contact rate × close rate).**

Worked examples (industry-benchmark math, 2026):

| Lead type | CPL | Close rate | Effective CPA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared web lead | $40 | ~2% | **~$2,000** |
| Exclusive web lead | $100 | ~10% | **~$1,000** |
| Live transfer call | $45 (×~50% qualified, close ~20%) | — | **~$450** |
| **Owned funnel lead (mature)** | blended ~$10–$40 all-in | 15–30% | **~$100–$250** |

The "cheap" $40 shared lead is the **most expensive** path to a client. ## 21. The Compliance Reality (TCPA / CAN-SPAM / CMS)

This is where agents get sued or fined into bankruptcy. Read this section twice.

### TCPA (calls & texts) — current status

- The FCC's **"one-to-one consent" rule was vacated** by the 11th Circuit in *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC* (Jan 24, 2025); the FCC later removed it and (per public reporting) stated in April 2025 it would not challenge the ruling. **Bundled consent is, for now, again permissible** at the federal rule level.
- **This is NOT "texting is now a free-for-all."** The underlying TCPA still requires **prior express written consent** for marketing calls/texts sent with an autodialer or prerecorded/artificial voice. The DNC registry still applies to texts. Revocation must be honored quickly.
- **Critically:** major carriers and texting platforms (and many courts) **still operationally require 1:1-style consent and proof** as a business rule, regardless of the vacated FCC rule. Plan to meet the higher standard anyway — it's the safe posture and the platforms enforce it.
- **Practical takeaway:** do not cold-text or cold-blast purchased lists. Only contact people who gave you clear, documented, written consent to be contacted *by you* about insurance. Use a consent-capture/documentation tool (e.g., TrustedForm) and keep records.

### CAN-SPAM (email)

Email is more permissive than texting but still regulated. For every commercial email: no false/misleading headers or subject lines; identify it as an ad where applicable; include a valid physical postal address; provide a clear, working opt-out and honor it within 10 business days; don't email harvested addresses. CAN-SPAM allows cold *email* in a way TCPA does not allow cold *texting* — but state laws and deliverability/reputation still constrain you, and Medicare adds rules below.

### CMS — Medicare is its own, stricter regime

Medicare marketing is governed by CMS on top of TCPA. Unsolicited contact rules, the prohibition on certain cold outreach to Medicare beneficiaries, scope-of-appointment requirements, recording/retention rules, and TPMO disclaimers all apply. **An inbound call where the beneficiary contacted you (e.g., from your funnel) is the clean path** — it establishes the 1:1 consent and avoids the unsolicited-contact problem. This is *why* inbound/live-transfer and an owned quiz funnel are strategically superior for Medicare specifically, not just economically.

> **The compliance-driven strategic conclusion:** an **inbound, consent-first owned funnel** isn't just cheaper — for Medicare it's the *legally cleanest* model. Build the funnel so the prospect always raises their hand first.

## 23. Is the 10-Question Quiz Funnel Compliant? How to Build It Right

**Short answer: yes, a quiz/questionnaire funnel is compliant and is one of the best models you can run — *if* you build consent in correctly.** It is not inherently a TCPA/CMS problem; a sloppy one is.

Why it works: it's inbound and self-initiated (the prospect chose to engage), it qualifies before you ever spend time, it delivers value (the result), and it's the natural place to capture *clear, written, documented* consent — exactly what TCPA wants and what makes Medicare contact clean.

**Length:** "10 questions" is fine but not magic. Best practice is **5–9 questions** — long enough to qualify and create investment, short enough to finish. Put the easy/engaging questions first, the contact + consent step last (after they're invested but before the result).

**A compliant senior-market quiz skeleton:**

1. What are you looking to do? (Lower my Medicare costs / Get final-expense coverage / Review my current policy / Plan for my family)
2. Age band
3. ZIP code (confirms Nevada licensure/service area)
4. Current coverage status
5. Health/eligibility light qualifier (no sensitive specifics stored beyond what you need)
6. Priority (lowest premium / best coverage / specific doctors / leave money for family)
7. Timeframe
8. Budget comfort band
9. **Contact step + consent:** name, email, phone — with an **unchecked, explicit consent checkbox** in plain language stating they agree to be contacted by *you/your agency by name* by phone, text, and email about insurance, that consent isn't a condition of purchase, message/data rates apply, and how to opt out (reply STOP / unsubscribe). Capture a consent timestamp + the exact language shown (use a TrustedForm-style certificate).
10. Result delivered (personalized recommendation / "your estimated savings range" / "your coverage snapshot") + booking calendar.

**Non-negotiables:**
- Consent box **unchecked by default**, separate from other agreements, in clear language, naming you/your agency specifically.
- Don't make consent a condition of getting the result/quote (TCPA best practice).
- Link a real privacy policy and, for Medicare, the required TPMO disclaimer.
- Log and store consent (who, when, exact wording, IP) — your defense if a complaint ever lands.
- For Medicare, the quiz should generate an **inbound** conversation — the cleanest CMS posture.
- Have the final consent language and Medicare disclaimers reviewed by a compliance attorney before launch. This one paragraph is the difference between an asset and a liability.

## 25. Text & Email Blasting — When It's Legal, When It's a Lawsuit

You asked specifically: is it better to set up text/email blasting (and stay CAN-SPAM compliant), or just buy leads?

**Direct answer:** Cold **text** blasting to purchased/unconsented lists is the single fastest way to get sued under the TCPA — statutory damages run per message and plaintiff firms troll for exactly this. Even with the one-to-one rule vacated, you still need prior express written consent for autodialed/mass marketing texts, the DNC registry still covers texts, and carriers/platforms will shut you down. **Do not cold-text bought lists. Ever.**

**Email** blasting is legally more survivable (CAN-SPAM permits cold commercial email with honest headers, an ID, a physical address, and a working opt-out honored within 10 business days), but cold-blast email converts poorly, wrecks domain reputation, and Medicare adds restrictions. It's low-ROI and reputationally risky as a primary strategy.

**The right way to "blast":** build a **consented list through your owned funnel** and then run high-frequency, automated, *permission-based* email + SMS nurture to people who explicitly opted in. That is legal, high-converting, and an owned asset. The tactic isn't "blasting vs. buying" — it's "blast a list you earned consent for" vs. "blast a list that gets you sued."

So, ranked for your question:
- **Best:** consent-based nurture to your owned funnel list (legal + high ROI + asset).
- **Good supplemental:** buying *direct* high-intent leads (live transfer/inbound, exclusive fresh) while the funnel matures.
- **Situational:** cheap aged leads for spring/summer pre-AEP nurture, worked compliantly by phone with proper consent posture.
- **Avoid:** cold text blasting unconsented/purchased lists (lawsuit), cold email blasting as a primary channel (low ROI, reputation), buying shared leads through an FMO markup (worst CPA).

## 29. KPIs & The One Number That Matters

Track weekly, by source:

- **Cost per acquisition (CPA) = cost per *issued* policy** — *the* number. Everything else is diagnostic.
- Cost per lead (CPL) — input only, never optimize on this alone.
- Contact rate (% of leads reached) — fix with cadence/speed before blaming lead quality.
- Quote rate, close rate, average annualized premium, persistency/chargeback rate.
- Funnel: traffic → quiz starts → quiz completes → consented contacts → booked → issued.
- Referrals generated per client, and renewal/retention rate (protects the book).

**Decision rule:** compute your **maximum allowable CPA** from your commission × close rate. Any source above it gets cut within 30 days. Any source below it gets more budget. Loyalty to a vendor or a channel is not a strategy — measured CPA is. The agents who win in 2026 aren't loyal to one source; they test systematically, track cost per issued policy by vendor, and scale only what consistently clears the profit bar.
